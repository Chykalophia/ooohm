Out Of Office, Holiday Manager

Time off, without the back‑and‑forth.

OOOhm is a calm leave tracker for small teams. Ask for days in one sentence, approve them from your inbox, and see who's out at a glance. There's no HR suite attached.

Run it yourself

Request time off

I’m taking PTO (Scheduled Time Off) from Mon, Dec 21 to Wed, Dec 23, full days .

This request takes 3 days. Afterwards you'd have 6 days left for 2026.

PTO for 2026: one square is one day. Filled is taken, striped is this request, empty is left.

Playing a few examples, counted Monday to Friday with US holidays. Change any part of the sentence to try your own.

How it works

Four screens. That’s the whole app for most people.

01

Ask in one sentence.

Pick a type and two dates. OOOhm counts the working days as you go and tells you what you’ll have left, before you send anything.

  • Half days are one choice in the sentence
  • Add a note for your approver if you like
  • Prefer labelled fields? An admin can switch the form

02

Approve with the whole picture.

Every request says who else is off on those days, or that nobody in the team is. Approve or decline in one click, with a reason if you want one. It’s on their dashboard straight away.

  • One approver per person: their manager
  • Needs you, then Settled, so nothing slips
  • Approve straight from the email, too

03

Or drag across the days.

On the calendar, press on the first day and let go on the last. Weekends and company holidays are hatched, and they never count against anyone.

  • Thanksgiving week is three days, not five
  • Or click the first day, then the last; the keyboard works too
  • Month and year views

04

See who’s out, and when.

One list for the month, filtered by team. Your own year on one page. And if you live in your calendar app, subscribe and it stays up to date there.

  • Pending requests are marked, so plans aren’t surprises
  • Feeds for Google Calendar, Apple Calendar and Outlook

Balances

A year of leave you can count on your fingers.

Each person gets a yearly allowance per leave type, and it resets on January 1. Days are counted to one decimal, so a half day is 0.5. Nothing is rounded behind anyone’s back.

What happens to the days nobody took is up to you, per leave type. Carryover is off unless you turn it on.

For managers

Say yes from wherever the request finds you.

Quiet by design

A few rules it never breaks.

  1. Invite-only.

    Nobody signs up on their own. An admin invites people, and they sign in with an emailed link, or with Google if you turn it on.

  2. Weekends and holidays never count.

    Set your working days and company holidays once. Add a holiday later and every request on that date is recounted, and the people affected get an email.

  3. Every change is on the record.

    Each decision and each balance change writes an audit row that names who made it. Export a person’s history as CSV before you ever delete them.

  4. One accent, one main action.

    Each screen does one thing. Pick a look for the whole company from nine themes and five layouts, including one with larger text and 48 px targets.

Developers

Every change is an event you can build on.

OOOhm writes a domain event in the same transaction as every change, so nothing happens that your other tools can’t hear about. Webhooks are signed and retried; the REST API reads everything else.

Webhooks
HMAC-signed, delivered at least once, with retries and a replay endpoint.
REST API
People, leave types, requests, balances, holidays and events, with keys you can revoke and an OpenAPI spec.
Calendar feeds
Personal and team ICS feeds for Google Calendar, Apple Calendar and Outlook.

Self-hosting

Runs where you do. Your data stays in your Postgres.

  • Docker Compose

    The database, the app, a background worker and a mail catcher, with migrations on start.

    docker compose up

  • Any Postgres 15+, or Vercel

    Your own server, Neon, Supabase or RDS. Pooled connections are detected for you. On Vercel, migrations run as part of the build.

    pnpm db:migrate && pnpm build && pnpm start

  • One machine, no database server

    Embedded Postgres in the same process, for a small team on a small box.

    pnpm start:embedded

Does it do payroll, reviews or onboarding?

No. It tracks time off, and nothing else. It sends events if you want your HR tools to know.

How many people is it for?

Small teams: one company per install, departments to group people, and one manager for each person.

What does a person need to sign in?

An invite from an admin. Sign-in is a link by email, or Google if you set it up. There are no passwords to forget.

Can people take half days?

Yes, the morning or the afternoon of a single day. It counts as 0.5.

Where is our data?

In the Postgres database you run it on, or in a folder on the machine in embedded mode. Nothing leaves your server except the emails people expect and the webhooks you add.
